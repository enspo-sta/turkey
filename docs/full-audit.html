Full audit · Hotrod Outdoor Alaska Fishing

Full Audit, All Fixed

Nine reviewers read the whole game, about 57,000 lines in 106 files. Each worked one area, and between them they reported 78 defects. 73 of these are fixed and pushed, and three are partly fixed. Two were not changed: one is a missing feature rather than a fault, the other a way of working.

Fixing introduced one crash of my own, caught before release by the check run on the final build; it is fixed and described below. All 17 regression scenarios pass on the final build, and the flows that were broken were driven again to check them. Parts were rewritten only where their structure kept causing bugs: the car's movement step, who owns a bear's threat, and one reset for each new game.

Method

How the audit was done

The code was split into nine areas, and one reviewer read each area in full:

  • fishing and money;
  • starting, saving and the menus;
  • touch input, the camera and the sky;
  • the player and the vehicles;
  • the animals and the visitor's story;
  • the drawing of the world;
  • world generation and the buildings;
  • the landmarks;
  • sound and the build.

A reviewer reported a defect only after tracing it through the code. Most also confirmed it by driving the game headless in Chromium, at several frame rates. Every report came with a concrete case: what you do, and what goes wrong.

I checked each finding against the code before fixing it. The fixes went out in seven commits on the branch. The checks at the end ran on the final build, dist/index.html, which is rebuilt from these sources.

What mattered most

The worst of what was found

  • A bear from the last game mauled the new player. Quit to the title while a grizzly was charging, then start a new game. The bear was still on your trail, and within 4 seconds the new player went from 100 to 0 health and lost 10% of their money. The same leftover charge could also pay out the $300 "Survive a grizzly charge" challenge to a fresh game. Shot animals stayed dead into the next game, and a shot bear could be claimed again for money. Now one reset at the start of every game puts each bear and animal back alive and at home, with nothing on your trail.
  • A car kept on the bridge came back at the bottom of the river. The save kept only where the car was across the map, not its height. On Continue, the car was placed on the river bed under 4 metres of water, where it could hardly move. Now the height is saved, and the car is placed from it.
  • Photos could quietly stop the game saving. The album shares the device's storage with the save. Once the album had filled it, every save failed without a word, and the Continue button could disappear. Now the camera roll's oldest pictures make room for the save. Continue no longer depends on a test write, and a save that still fails says so.
  • Tapping a hold did nothing on a phone. Climbing asks you to tap the next hold, but a tap on the view was never registered on touch screens.
  • Roads did not meet at their junctions. At the Springs junction one road was buried 2 metres in a gravel bank while the other floated 2 metres above the ground. The same happened, by about a metre, at three other junctions. Roads now meet at one height.
  • The race car drove through trees at 30 frames a second. At top speed it moved 2.9 metres a frame, further than its collision check could see. The car now moves in steps of under a metre.
Rewrites

Which parts were rewritten, and which were not

A rewrite was done only where one structure lay behind several findings.

  • The car's movement (src/entities/car.js): each frame's move is now a series of steps under a metre long. Each step checks grip, deep water, collisions and cliffs. This fixed driving through trees, crossing narrow deep channels in one frame, and grip and crash braking that changed with the frame rate.
  • Bears (src/gameplay/bears.js): one bear at a time can be after you, and it ends its threat itself. Before, any bear's charge could end another bear's threat, a second wounded bear could take it over, and a fishing bear came back as a roaming one. There is also now one reset per game.
  • One reset for each new game (resetSession in src/main.js). Several things belong to a game and not to its save, and some were reset in some flows only, some in none: bears, animals, fairy luck, bug dope, tonight's alerts, the view's zoom and the tor's flake. Every new or continued game now starts from the same state. Fades and other delayed work also check that their game is still the one being played.
  • The shadow hooks (src/world/shadowbatch.js, src/entities/wildlife.js): both put themselves back after a lost graphics context.
  • Not rewritten:
    • the menus' navigation, which a reviewer suggested turning into a stack;
    • the many ways a fishing attempt can end;
    • the save's field-by-field loading.
    Their bugs are fixed in place, and none of the three is causing a bug now. Rewriting them would risk new bugs for no change you could see.

The picture: the last round's optimisations still exact

tools/same-frame.mjs drew the same moment with each of the last round's frame-rate optimisations on and off, in all ten views. Every picture had 0 differing pixels.

A crash I introduced, and caught

Part 2 of the fixes moved the car's movement into steps. The exhaust and dust code further down still used the heading's sine and cosine, which had been worked out in the moved block. So the game threw an error on every frame in which a car moved. This was pushed in part 2, found by the drive check before release, and fixed in part 6. The game was not republished between those two commits.

Roads at their junctions

The number of road sections whose ribbon is more than 0.35 m off the ground fell from 51 to 28. All 22 left on the Coast Road are the bridge deck, which is meant to stand above the river bed. The other 6 are road edges at most 0.73 m off. Five of them are at the Springs junction, where the ground under one road's edge belongs to the other road's verge; the sixth is one edge of the River Road, 0.37 m off.

Limits

What this report cannot show

  • Nothing was tested on a real iPhone. The checks ran in headless Chromium with a software renderer on a desktop processor, at an iPhone's screen size. Safari on iOS, its touch handling and its graphics chip were not used.
  • Nine reviewers do not find everything. Each read one area in full, but bugs that only appear between areas, or only after hours of play, can remain.
  • Some fixes change what you see, on purpose:
    • roads at the four junctions;
    • the positions of the junction signpost, the Glacier Lake campfire and the kayak;
    • the start of Halibut Pier;
    • tree shadows that now reach into view at a low sun;
    • particles that keep their size while zoomed.
    The previous round's pixel-for-pixel guarantee covered only that round's own changes.
  • The sources below could not be read again from here. Outbound requests from this environment were blocked: curl got a refusal from the network proxy. The claims about three.js were checked in its source as installed with the game.
What would change this conclusion

What would change this conclusion

  • A bug report from a real phone in any of the fixed flows: quitting and starting again, Continue, saving with photos, touch taps, or driving. That would mean a fix did not reach the case it was meant for. The headless checks imitate these flows; they are not the device.
  • The game running out of storage on a phone even with the roll emptied. That would mean the album alone fills the storage. The fix then is to move the photos to the browser's database (IndexedDB) and keep web storage for the save only, as a reviewer suggested.
  • Frame time going up at sunset. Trees whose shadows reach into the view are now drawn even when they themselves are out of view. That costs some drawing at a low sun. If a trace on the phone shows it, the shadow reach (120 m) can be shortened.
  • New code that moves an object between the water's reflection and the view. The one-matrix-update optimisation from the last round assumes nothing does. Both the reviewers and I checked that this holds today, but nothing enforces it.
