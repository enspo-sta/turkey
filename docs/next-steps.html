Done now

A river boulder that looked like glass

Catch pictures at Hotrod Landing showed a pale cyan block in the water beside the fish. It was a boulder: of the 180 that lie in water, three had their tops less than 0.7 m under the surface, and one of them lies 27 m from where you start. Light grey stone, under clear shallow water with bright caustics playing on it, showed as flat pale facets like glass.

Wet stone is darker than dry: the film of water traps light against the surface until more of it is absorbed (Lekner and Dorf, in the sources). The game already darkened the wet band just above the waterline; stone under the surface now gets the same darkening, so a submerged boulder reads as a rock under the water rather than a pale block. Dry rock, the water, the riverbed and everything else are unchanged; no rock moved.

Settling the boulder deeper was tried first, 0.8 m and then 2 m down: it stayed a pale block, only smaller, so that change was dropped. All seven water views of the regression set (Hotrod Landing, Salmon Bend, Bear Falls, Moose Lake, Glacier Lake, Halibut Pier and Moose Lake at sunset) were drawn before and after: the one visible change was the boulder at the landing (the mist differs between runs, as it follows the weather), and no shader reported an error.

Done now

A photo taken upright frames every fish whole

The album kept every picture at 16 by 9. Held upright, an iPhone 15 is 393 by 852 points, so the picture was a band 338 by 190 points across the middle of the screen, and a big fish held up for a trophy shot did not fit: measured on the build before this change, every Big Skate lost about a quarter (23.4% of the smallest, 123 cm, and 24.4% of the largest, 241 cm), and the biggest halibut 5.1% (218 cm, and Barn Door, the legendary halibut, at 232 cm).

A picture taken with the screen upright is now 4 by 3: a frame 338 by 253 points, kept at 280 by 210 pixels. That is about as many pixels as the 16 by 9 picture (320 by 180), so the album takes about the same room on the phone: in the camera check the stored album was 22 KB upright against 20 KB on its side, for the same two subjects. With the phone on its side, and on a tablet or a computer held wide, nothing changes.

The stars a picture earns are measured against a 16 by 9 frame as wide (189 points tall in the check, in a frame 335 by 252), so an upright picture is no harder to fill than before: the moose 30 m away earned 3 stars upright and on its side. The viewfinder shows the taller frame, and its ● PHOTO label now sits at the top in the middle when upright, where it no longer runs under the RUN button. In the album each picture shows whole in its tile, an upright one with dark bars at its sides; tapped, it shows big as it was taken.

The catch check now fails if any of the 71 fish is cut by the photo frame on any of its nine shapes; before, it only counted them.

Measured, left as it is

A shorter catch card would not have helped

Upright, the catch card takes a third of the screen (279 of 852 points, measured on this build), leaving the fish 333 points above it. The plan was to make the card shorter upright, so the biggest fish could be held nearer and look bigger. Measured first, by trying all 71 fish against the room a shorter card would leave:

In full screen, 2 of 71 fish come nearer, by 7%, however much shorter the card is. Held upright, a long fish is limited by the width of the screen, not by the card: 39 of the 71 are held further out than usual on a screen 393 points wide, and a shorter card cannot change that. Only with Safari's bars showing does it help, and then for 5 of the 71 fish. So the card keeps its text and buttons at the size they are.

What would change this conclusion

What would change this conclusion

The conclusion: the next step is yours, a catch tried on your iPhone, on its side and upright, then your pick of the three options; I would start with the mounted fish. This would change if:

  • Your iPhone shows a fish under the card or out of the picture, or the hand still wrong. That comes first, from your screenshot.
  • You play upright in Safari with its bars showing. Then a shorter card on short screens would bring 5 of the 71 fish nearer, by up to 1.22 times for a card 60 points shorter.
  • Your frame rate reading is low. Then a round for speed comes before anything new.
  • You want the game in the App Store now. Then the publishing round comes first; the guide lists every step.
  • The boulder looks too dark, or still glassy, on your phone. The darkening is one number: stone under water keeps 0.68 of its colour, as the wet band just above the waterline does at its wettest.
