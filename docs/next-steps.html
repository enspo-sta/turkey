A river boulder that looked like glass

Catch pictures at Hotrod Landing showed a pale cyan block in the water beside the fish. It was a boulder: of the 180 that lie in water, three had their tops less than 0.7 m under the surface, and one of them lies 27 m from where you start. Light grey stone, under clear shallow water with bright caustics playing on it, showed as flat pale facets like glass.

Wet stone is darker than dry: the film of water traps light against the surface until more of it is absorbed (Lekner and Dorf, in the sources). The game already darkened the wet band just above the waterline; stone under the surface now gets the same darkening, so a submerged boulder reads as a rock under the water rather than a pale block. Dry rock, the water, the riverbed and everything else are unchanged; no rock moved.

Settling the boulder deeper was tried first, 0.8 m and then 2 m down: it stayed a pale block, only smaller, so that change was dropped. All seven water views of the regression set (Hotrod Landing, Salmon Bend, Bear Falls, Moose Lake, Glacier Lake, Halibut Pier and Moose Lake at sunset) were drawn before and after: the one visible change was the boulder at the landing (the mist differs between runs, as it follows the weather), and no shader reported an error.

The catch check finds new fish by itself

The new catch-room scenario held up a list of 71 fish written out when it was made. It now builds the list from the game's own fish tables, so a species or a legendary fish added later is checked without touching the scenario. On the new build it found the same 71 fish and passed all ten of its lines.

What would change this conclusion

What would change this conclusion

The conclusion: the next step is yours, a catch tried on your iPhone, on its side and upright. Until then, the compact card and the taller upright photo are the best use of a round, as both finish what your last report started. This would change if:

  • Your iPhone shows a fish under the card, or the hand still wrong. That comes first, from your screenshot.
  • You want the game in the App Store now. Then the publishing round comes first; the guide lists every step.
  • The boulder looks too dark, or still glassy, on your phone. The darkening is one number: stone under water keeps 0.68 of its colour, as the wet band just above the waterline does at its wettest.

Built on the claude/ruben-hotrod-fishing branch: the code on GitHub, the playable build and the catch card report. The boulder fix is in src/world/scatter.js (the rock material); the fish tables for tests are window.__rhf.data in src/main.js.
