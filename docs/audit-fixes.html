Fixes · Hotrod Outdoor Alaska Fishing

Fixing the Audit

Every problem the first-principles audit found that code can fix is now fixed and checked. The one it cannot fix, the missing measurement from your phone, now takes one tap: Settings, Check performance, then copy the line it gives you and send it to me.

What you will notice:

  • The graphics no longer drop to Low for good. A phone held at 30 frames a second by Low Power Mode keeps High. If your settings were left on Low by the old adaptation, the game starts on High again.
  • A new Check performance button in Settings. It measures about 15 seconds of play and says what holds the frame rate back.
  • No false bear alarm. A bear with no fish to smell, or with you in the car, no longer sounds the alarm and makes you drop your line. The new checks found this game bug.
  • In the iPhone app the game follows the silent switch. In Safari it still plays through it.

Finding 2 and 5

The adaptation, rebuilt

The graphics have two dials: the resolution, from twice the screen's points down to one, and the preset, from your choice down to Low. Frame times are averaged over windows of 2.5 seconds.

  • Below 50 frames a second, the adaptation tries one dial at its cheapest setting, the resolution first. The change is a trial, kept only if the frames come at least 8% quicker. If they do not, the change is undone and the other dial is tried. If neither helps, the limit is not the game's work (a cap, or a hot phone), so the picture is left alone and no step down is tried for a while: first one minute, then two, up to ten.
  • Why the cheapest setting first. A 60 Hz screen shows frames on whole refreshes. A frame that takes 21 ms instead of 24 still waits for the next refresh, so a quarter step down can make the work cheaper and still show no quicker frame. Trying the cheapest setting answers "does this dial help at all?". If it does, the steps back up find the best setting that keeps the frames smooth.
  • Above 57 frames a second for 10 seconds, one step back up, as a trial. It is undone if the frames then drop below 50, and each failed try waits longer before the next.
  • Your choice is never changed. The level the adaptation settles on is remembered for the next game separately from your choice. Settings lights your choice and says when the game is running below it, for example "Running now: HIGH at 1.25× resolution".
  • Old settings are released once. The old adaptation saved its step down as your choice. Settings saved before this version with Low or Medium therefore start on High again. If you had picked Medium by hand, pick it again.

Findings 1 and 3

The performance check

Settings, Check performance, during a game. Stand still and look ahead for about 15 seconds. The check records, frame by frame, the time from one frame to the next and the game's own work on each. It does this first at your graphics as they are, then for 5 seconds at the lowest resolution. Then it puts everything back. The verdict:

  • Full speed: about 60 frames a second. Nothing to fix.
  • The graphics chip: fewer pixels made the frames at least 10% quicker.
  • The processor: the game's own work alone takes at least 15 ms, nine tenths of a frame at 60 a second, or three quarters of each frame, and fewer pixels did not help.
  • A cap: frames held at a steady 30 a second while the game's work is under 12 ms, and fewer pixels did not help. Low Power Mode is the usual cause; a hot phone is another.
  • Not clear: none of these. A hot phone is the likeliest cause.

The screen ends in one line to copy, holding the frame rate, the time per frame, the game's work, the fewer-pixels result, the graphics, the screen, the verdict, the sound's state and the iPhone's system version. For example, this line came from a recorded pattern in the headless browser, which is why it names Linux and no audio session:

Hotrod check 2026-10-09 20:29 UTC · 30.0 fps (1% low 30) · frame 33.3 ms, game work 8.2 ms (25%) · steady 30: 100% · at 1.00x: 30.0 fps · running HIGH 2.00x of HIGH · screen 852x393 @2 · verdict: cap · sound: running, session none, in the browser · Linux x86_64

Each verdict was tested on recorded patterns, inside the built game: full speed; the graphics chip; the processor, with and without the 60 Hz rounding; the cap; unclear; and too few frames. The check was also run end to end in the built game. It held the adaptation, dropped to 1.00× for the comparison, put 2.00× back, released the adaptation and showed the result screen, and Copy worked.

Findings 7 and 8

Checks that fail

Each value the 19 regression scenarios read now carries what it must be. That makes 172 such checks, plus 7 that read the game directly. Three helpers wrote them for the 17 existing scenarios from the last good run's values and the game's code, asserting what the game should do rather than what it did. I wrote the two new scenarios. Writing and running the checks found real problems on the first day:

  • A game bug: the false bear alarm. A stalking bear raised the alarm before it checked whether there was any fish to smell, then turned away in the same frame. You got the banner, the growl, the announcer, "Switch to your bow!" and a dropped line, for a bear that was leaving. In play this happens when you get into the car, or your cooler is empty, with a stalker within 42 m. The bear now turns away first. The bear scenario checks that no alarm sounds and no line is dropped.
  • A scenario bug: the summit register. The climbing scenario meant to stand the player by the register, but put them 0.12 m off the summit's stand, so they fell 15 m to the ground. Its picture never showed the register. It now places the player on the stand.
  • A harness bug: waits that let no time pass. On the software renderer, with three scenarios at once, a frame takes several seconds. A "wait three seconds" step could pass without a single frame, so the old scenarios compared values from before anything had happened. In the regression scenarios a wait now also lasts until the game's own clock has moved on as far, and a new frames step waits for drawn frames.

Limits

What this does not show

  • Nothing was tested on a real iPhone. Every run was headless Chromium with a software renderer. The adaptation was tested with modelled phones and the check with recorded patterns. Until the line comes from your phone, how the game behaves there is still a prediction.
  • The game's own work, as the check measures it, may include waits for the graphics chip. When the graphics chip is the limit, the browser can make the game's drawing calls wait. That is why the check tries fewer pixels first: if fewer pixels help, the verdict is the graphics chip whatever the work reads.
  • A trial shows for a few seconds. When frames are slow, the adaptation may show the lowest resolution for 5 seconds, or Low for about 7, to learn whether it helps. On a capped phone that happens at most every few minutes, and then every ten.
  • Safari's 30-frame cap in Low Power Mode is still recalled, not re-read. The fix does not depend on it: any cap or hot phone is now handled the same way.
  • Medium picked by hand is released once, along with the Low the old adaptation saved.
What would change this conclusion

What would change this conclusion

  • Your check saying "full speed" or "cap". Then the game's work was never the problem on your phone, and the frame-rate complaints came from the cap or the old stuck Low. Both are fixed now.
  • Your check saying "the processor". Then the next step is far fewer draw calls (merging static objects, drawing far scenery as pictures), measured against the budget. Or a cheaper preset is the honest answer, and the adaptation now finds it by itself.
  • Your check saying "the graphics chip". Then the adaptation's lower resolution is the right tool. The next work would be cheaper bloom, sun rays and shadows at full resolution.
  • The adaptation's trials being noticeable and annoying in play. Then the trials should wait longer, or be shown as a choice in Settings rather than tried.
  • A check failing on a value that is right. Then that check is wrong and should be fixed. The checks were written by reading the code, and some may be stricter than the game needs to be.
