How to publish the game

Share the link you already have today, give the game its own address on a Swedish host in an evening, and set aside 99 USD a year (about 1 000 kr) plus a few evenings of paperwork for the App Store.

Checked 30 September 2026. Apple’s requirements were read in full on Apple’s own pages; the web hosts and game portals were checked through search results because this build environment’s network rules block their sites (sources at the bottom).

Route 1The link you already have

The game page is shared as “Anyone with the link”, so you can send claude.ai/artifact/3urJEG61Boov67ViABm26A to anyone now. It opens in any browser on a phone, tablet or computer. Each player’s progress is saved in their own browser. Sharing is controlled from the page’s Share menu.

Route 2Your own web address on statichost.eu

statichost is a Swedish, fully European static site host with a free plan (100 build minutes and 10 GB of traffic a month). The game is one 1.2 MB page plus icons, so no server is needed.

  1. Get the game onto your repository’s main branch: on GitHub, open a pull request from claude/ruben-hotrod-fishing to main and merge it.
  2. Create a free account at statichost.eu and add a site that points to your repository, github.com/enspo-sta/turkey. The repository is public, so no access key should be needed; statichost’s docs cover connecting a repository.
  3. statichost reads statichost.yml, which is already in the repository. It contains public: dist, which publishes the finished game in the dist folder unchanged, with no build step (build configuration docs).
  4. You get an address on statichost’s domain. You can connect a domain of your own later in its settings.
  5. To publish an update: run npm run build, commit and push, then deploy again from statichost (its docs describe automatic deploys).

The privacy policy is published with the game at /privacy.html on that address, which is the link the App Store needs. On iPhone and iPad, players open your address in Safari and choose Share, then Add to Home Screen. The game then starts full screen from its own icon.

Not recommended here: Codeberg Pages (Germany) is free, but Codeberg’s terms now exclude projects written mostly by artificial intelligence tools, which this game is. GitHub Pages is based in the United States and would need an extra deployment workflow to serve the dist folder.

Route 3European web game portals

Run npm run zip to package the game as ruben-hotrod-fishing-web.zip (0.6 MB, with index.html at the top, as the portals expect).

CrazyGames (Leuven, Belgium)

  • Upload the zip in the CrazyGames developer portal; their quality team usually answers within one to two days.
  • A “Basic Launch” puts the game on the site without any extra code, to see how players respond. Games that hold players’ attention are invited to a “Full Launch”, which needs the CrazyGames software kit for ads and is where you start earning.
  • No fee and no exclusivity. Size limit for the first download is 50 MB (this game is 0.6 MB). Payouts are monthly once your balance reaches 100 €.

Poki (Amsterdam, Netherlands)

  • Every game is reviewed by people and then tested with players in stages before a worldwide release.
  • Requirements: the Poki software kit built in (gameplay start and stop signals, ad breaks), a first download under 8 MB, and a 16:9 picture that scales on computers and phones. This game would need that code work before submitting.
  • Ad revenue is split 50/50 on visitors Poki brings, and you keep all of it on visitors you bring yourself.

Route 4The App Store, step by step

Before you start
  • A Mac with Xcode 26 or later. Apple requires Xcode 26 to upload iPhone and iPad apps.
  • An Apple Account with two-factor authentication. You then enroll in the Apple Developer Program as an individual: 99 USD per membership year, charged in kronor or euro during enrollment (about 950–1 050 kr or 85–92 € depending on the exchange rate). Your legal name is shown as the seller on the App Store.
  • A web address for the privacy policy (route 2 gives you /privacy.html), and a support page with a way to reach you. Apple requires the support page to show actual contact information, such as an email address.
  1. Enroll on Apple’s enrollment page or in the Apple Developer app, and wait for approval.
  2. Accept the agreements in App Store Connect under Business. You cannot add an app before the latest agreement is signed. A paid app also needs the Paid Apps agreement, bank details and tax forms.
  3. Declare your EU trader status under Business, Agreements, Compliance, Digital Services Act. For a free hobby game, “This is not a trader account” is likely right. Charging for the game or showing ads points toward being a trader, and then your address, phone number and email are shown publicly on the App Store page. Apple states that it cannot decide this for you.
  4. Prepare the build. In the repository run npm run build, open ios/RubenHotrodFishing.xcodeproj, choose your team under Signing & Capabilities, and set a bundle identifier nobody else uses, for example se.yourname.rubenhotrodfishing. Version 1.0, build 1 are already set.
  5. Create the app record: Apps, the + button, New App. Choose iOS, name Ruben Hotrod Fishing (at most 30 characters, and the name must be free on the store), primary language English, the bundle identifier from Xcode, and an internal SKU such as ruben-hotrod-fishing-1.
  6. Upload: in Xcode choose the destination “Any iOS Device”, then Product, Archive. In the window that opens, choose Distribute App, then App Store Connect, and follow the prompts to upload (Apple’s guide). Apple processes the build and emails you when it appears.
  7. Test with TestFlight (recommended): up to 100 people on your team, and up to 10 000 outside testers by email or a public link once the first build passes a short review.
  8. Fill in the product page using the table below.
  9. Submit: on the version page, check the Build section, click Add for Review, then Submit for Review. The status changes to In Review.
  10. Release by hand or automatically after approval. For later updates, raise the version in Xcode, archive, upload and submit again.

Review risks and how the game meets them
  • Guideline 4.2, minimum functionality: apps that are only a repackaged website get rejected. This is a complete game bundled inside the app. It runs offline and uses native vibration feedback. If a reviewer questions it, reply in App Review with exactly that.
  • Guideline 2.3.8: screenshots must suit all ages, for example no weapon pointed at a character. The ready-made screenshots show no hunting.
  • Guideline 5.1.1: the privacy policy must be linked in App Store Connect and reachable inside the app. Both are in place.

CaveatsWhat would change this conclusion

  • An App Store rejection under guideline 4.2 would leave routes 1 to 3 open; the next steps would be an appeal through App Review, or adding more native features to the app.
  • Wanting income changes the paperwork: a price or ads makes you an EU trader with public contact details, and needs Apple’s paid agreement, bank and tax details. On the web it means building in the CrazyGames or Poki software kit.
  • More than about 10 000 plays a month would outgrow statichost’s free 10 GB of traffic; its paid plans or another European host would take over.
  • The dollar exchange rate moves the 99 USD fee in kronor and euro; Apple shows the exact amount at checkout.
  • Apple changing its rules (Xcode version, screenshot sizes, age rating tiers) would change the steps; the linked Apple pages are the ones to re-check.
