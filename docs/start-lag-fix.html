Hotrod Outdoor Alaska Fishing · fix report

Start Lag Fix

On a processor slowed four times, as a stand-in for a phone, the first ten seconds of a game averaged 28.3 ms a frame in the build the last round shipped (24.5 ms in the build before that round; medians of three runs each). With this fix they average 22.1 ms (−22%), the frames over 50 ms fell from 10 to 4, and the tap that starts a game went from 1,400 ms to 158 ms. The sounds and Tesla’s statue are now made off the main thread, the tap no longer rebuilds what has not changed, and the first moments of a game can no longer push the graphics down for good.

Reported

“Game is laggy at least in the beginning”

The report came straight after the sound round, which added a library of 66 sounds made in code when the game starts. That work was the first suspect, so the start of a game was measured before anything was changed, in three builds: the one before the sound round, the sound round as shipped, and later this fix.

How it was measured

A new tool, tools/start-lag.mjs, loads a build in the headless browser and slows the page’s processor down four times with the browser’s own throttling (the closest stand-in for a phone available here). It shows the title screen for three seconds, starts a game as a tap does and plays 45 seconds standing on the riverbank. It steps the game one frame at a time at a steady 1/60 s and times each frame’s own work: how much of it was sounds being made, sounds being handed to the browser and the statue being carved, each part counted once. Between frames the page gets the rest of the 1/60 s, so workers and messages run as they would.

The game was not simply left to run on its own clock, because the headless browser draws with software and far too slowly for that. Even with nothing happening at all, this build managed 13 frames in the 3 seconds on the title screen and 5 in the next 8 seconds. A first attempt that let the game run on its own showed no frames at all for 17 to 19 seconds after the tap in both builds tried that way, the one before the sound round and the shipped one: that is the renderer, not the game. The draw calls themselves are skipped, as in the frame-time tour (tools/perf-tour.mjs), so what is timed is the processor’s side of each frame.

Changed

What the fix does

  • The sound’s worker (src/audio/oven.js, src/workers/sound.js). The ambience’s textures, the library’s sounds and both reverbs’ impulses are made in a worker of their own: a background thread with its own processor time, from the moment the page starts. In the full-speed measurement, with the tap 3.0 s after the title screen came up, the worker had everything made at 2.2 s, before the tap. Finished sounds are handed to the browser one a frame for the ambience and one for the library, since making their buffers is the one part that must happen on the main thread. The two reverbs are handed over inside the tap, whose frame is long anyway, rather than as a stutter a moment into play. A reverb’s impulse must be at the sound context’s own rate, which is only known at the tap, so the worker makes each at the two usual rates (48 000 and 44 100 samples a second) and the other is let go. The worker closes once everything is made. Where a browser cannot start a worker, the sounds are made on the main thread a little a frame, as before.
  • The statue in the sculptor’s worker (src/world/teslacore.js, src/workers/sculpt.js). The worker that already carves the scientists’ busts now carves Tesla’s statue too. The statue’s code moved into a module of its own that touches nothing of the page, so the worker can load it. The statue comes back identical: 24,789 vertices, 43,954 triangles, and the same weighted sums over its positions, normals, colours and triangle corners as the statue carved on the main thread, in this build and in the shipped one. The worker uses three.js’s core alone; its renderer half, unused there, would have more than doubled the worker. The worker’s code is named in one place (src/workers/url.js), so the game carries it once.
  • Nothing rebuilt that has not changed. The arms’ look, the hot rod driver’s look and the hot rod’s paint each remember what they were last set to, and setting the same again does nothing (src/entities/viewmodel.js, src/entities/hotrod.js).
  • A game settles in before the graphics are stepped down (Game.settleIn in src/game.js). For the first 20 seconds of a game, slow frames do not lower the graphics preset; the resolution can still adapt. A phone that stays slow is still stepped down after that: with frames at 30 a second throughout, the fixed build stepped down at 25.0 s (high to medium) and 32.5 s (medium to low), none of it in the first 20 seconds.

The sound

First the sounds themselves. Every sound the game makes ahead of time (the 66 sounds of the library with all their takes, the ambience’s textures and footsteps, and both reverbs’ impulses: 89 sets of samples in all) was summed sample by sample, each sample with a weight of its own, in the shipped build, in this build with its worker and in this build given no worker. Every sum is the same in all three, to the sixth decimal, with the same lengths, channels and rates: the worker makes the same samples the main thread made, and the shipped build made the same.

Then as the player hears them: takes from the sound sheet (tools/sound-sheet.mjs) recorded from this build and compared with the round’s own recording of the shipped build. Most sounds have several takes and one is picked at random each time a sound plays, and some take in more by chance (which geese call, how far off the wolves are, which stretch of a song is caught), so the shipped build was recorded a second time to see how much a take moves by chance alone. Between its own two recordings the shipped build’s takes moved by up to 2.3 dB; this build’s are within 1.9 dB of the first recording.

The sound engine also ran 20 simulated minutes on the strict imitation of the browser’s audio from the sound round’s review (there is no worker there, so the sounds were made on the main thread, as the fallback does): no errors, no sound stopped twice, and all 66 sounds and the music’s hall made. Size

The game file is 3.04 MB, 173 KB more than as shipped (54 KB more compressed with gzip). The sound’s worker carries its own copy of the sound code (35 KB), and the sculptor’s worker now carries three.js’s core for the statue (150 KB, up from 16 KB).

Limits

What this report cannot show

  • Nothing was tested on a real iPhone. A desktop processor slowed four times stands in for a phone; a phone’s processor and memory behave differently, and its graphics chip was not measured at all.
  • The drawing is not in these numbers. The draw calls are skipped and the headless browser draws with software, so how long a phone’s graphics chip takes per frame, and any stall from sending new textures to it, are not measured.
  • The first frames after the tap are still the heaviest. The view, the ground round you and the interface all settle in then: the first frame took 116.5 ms with the processor slowed four times (35.8 ms at full speed), against 133.1 ms as shipped and 162.0 ms before the sound round.
  • A browser that cannot start a worker makes the sounds and the statue on the main thread a little a frame, as before this fix.
  • The sources below could not be read again from here. Outbound requests from this environment were blocked, so the references are cited from knowledge of them, not re-read today.
What would change this conclusion

What would change this conclusion

  • Lag at the start that a player still feels on a phone after this fix would point at what these numbers cannot see: the graphics chip, or sending the world’s textures to it. A trace on the phone itself (the timeline in Safari’s Web Inspector) would show it.
  • A phone that is slow from the start and stays slow now keeps its higher graphics for the first 20 seconds of a game before stepping down. If that start feels worse than the old early step-down, the number in settleIn is the one to change.
  • A browser in which the workers start late or run slowly, such as an old phone with few processor cores, would have the sounds arrive later. The game plays without them until then, but the place’s sound and the music would come in late. On a phone with only two cores, the workers of the first seconds (the sound’s, the statue’s and one for the busts) also share them with the game itself.
  • Memory: the made sounds now wait from the title screen until the tap (24.9 MB of samples, the same as before for a player who stayed on the title screen until everything was made). A phone short of memory at that moment would show it.
